# How to organize Chrome browser history by website

Chrome history is chronological by default. That is useful for answering “what did I open at 3 PM yesterday?” but less useful for answering “what websites do I actually use?”

If you open 30 pages on one website, ordinary history can show 30 separate entries. The underlying information is useful, but the presentation makes the site itself harder to see.

## A website-first history model

A website-first history manager groups page visits under their website or domain. The first level becomes a list of sites, while individual pages remain available when you open a site.

This makes several tasks easier:

- see recently used websites without repeated page titles taking over the list;
- sort sites by frequency or name;
- pin important sites;
- search old pages inside one specific site;
- attach notes and tags to the site rather than every individual visit.

## Why this can be easier to remember

Human memory often retains context better than exact metadata. You may forget a page title but remember that it was “on a design site,” “inside an AI tool,” or “on that shopping website.”

BestHistory is built around that pattern. It first turns raw page visits into a website library, then lets you drill down into the pages you visited.

## Keep the timeline when you need it

Website-first organization does not mean deleting chronological history. BestHistory also keeps a timeline, but consecutive pages from the same website can be collapsed into a group so repeated browsing does not flood the screen.

## Add lightweight organization instead of building another archive

Tags, notes, pinning and a wastebasket can be enough. The goal is not to manually classify every browsing event. It is to create a few extra paths back to the websites you are likely to need again.
